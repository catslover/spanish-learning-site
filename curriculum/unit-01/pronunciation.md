## Learner Goals

By the end of U1.1, Victor and Spencer should be able to:

1. Pronounce the five Spanish vowels with stable, short sounds.
2. Say common consonant patterns from HSA Lesson 1 clearly enough for a partner to recognize.
3. Notice stress and accent marks when reading short words aloud.
4. Compare meaningful regional variation without treating one native variety as the only correct one.
5. Practice with each other through modeled, scaffolded, and unscripted partner rounds.

## Core Pronunciation Content

### Stable Vowels

Spanish vowels are short and steady.

| Letter | Practice Sound | Practice Words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a | open `a` | mamá, casa, gracias |
| e | clear `e` | mesa, Elena, tres |
| i | clear `i` | mi, familia, sí |
| o | round `o` | hola, dos, profesor |
| u | round `u` | tú, uno, mucho |

Listen on Website marker: vowel set `a, e, i, o, u`.

### High-Value Consonants And Letter Patterns

| Pattern | Learner Note | Practice Words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ñ | One sound, as in `señor` | niño, español, mañana |
| j / ge / gi | Strong breathy sound in many native varieties | José, gente, gigante |
| h | Silent | hola, hermano, ahora |
| ll / y | Often similar in many regions | yo, llamo, familia |
| r / rr | Tap `r`; trill `rr` | pero, perro, gracias |
| qu | `k` sound before e/i | que, quiero, quince |
| gue / gui | hard `g` sound | Miguel, guitarra, guerra |
| ce / ci / z | Latin America: `s`; much of Spain: `th` | gracias, cinco, zapato |

Listen on Website marker: consonant patterns.

### Regional Variation To Label

- `z`, `ce`, and `ci`: In Latin America, these are commonly pronounced like `s`. In much of Spain, they are pronounced like English `th` in `think`.
- `ll` and `y`: In many regions, these sound very similar. Some regions pronounce them with a stronger `zh` or `sh` quality.
- `j`, `ge`, and `gi`: The strength of the breathy sound varies by region and speaker.

Instructional stance: students should recognize variation and choose one clear, consistent pronunciation for practice.

## Website Lesson Flow

1. Warm up with the five vowels.
2. Listen and repeat key consonant patterns.
3. Read short HSA-aligned words aloud.
4. Complete a partner ladder:
   - Modeled: both students repeat after the website.
   - Scaffolded: Student A reads; Student B chooses the pattern heard.
   - Unscripted: students spell or pronounce a name, place, or family word from memory.
5. Finish with a quick self-check.

## Partner Practice Script

Use the names Victor and Spencer in the directions so the activity is ready for them.

### Round 1: Modeled

Victor and Spencer listen to each item, then both repeat.

Items: `a`, `e`, `i`, `o`, `u`, `niño`, `hola`, `gracias`, `quiero`, `familia`.

### Round 2: Scaffolded

Victor reads one word. Spencer identifies the focus pattern. Then switch roles.

Words: `niño`, `José`, `hola`, `llamo`, `perro`, `que`, `guitarra`, `cinco`.

### Round 3: Unscripted

Each student chooses:

- one family word,
- one greeting,
- one name or place,
- one word with a pattern that still feels tricky.

The partner listens and gives one correction or one confirmation.

## Student Self-Check

Students mark each statement yes / almost / not yet:

- I can say the five vowels without changing them into English vowel sounds.
- I can pronounce `ñ`, silent `h`, `qu`, and `gue/gui`.
- I can explain one regional variation in Spanish pronunciation.
- I can read a short Spanish word aloud and ask my partner to check it.

## Parent / Instructor / Facilitator Reference

This is the single facilitator reference for the future U1.1 printable module.

### What To Listen For

- Vowels should stay short and steady.
- Silent `h` should not be pronounced.
- `qu` should sound like `k`, not like English `kw`.
- `gue/gui` should keep a hard `g`.
- `r` and `rr` may take time; accept improvement and consistency before perfection.

### Correction Routine

1. Praise the word or sound that was clear.
2. Isolate the sound once.
3. Have the student repeat the word in a short phrase.
4. Return to partner practice quickly.

### Completion Standard

The workshop is complete when both students can read the practice words aloud, identify the target pattern in a partner's word, and explain that regional pronunciation differences can be meaningful without being mistakes.
